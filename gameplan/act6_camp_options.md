# Act 6: a new place for the night stop (options, for Simon)

Act 6 is the race back to Cairo: the Keepers' clocks are running, every day costs both countdowns, and on the night stop the guardian's servants come out of the dark for the party round its fire (act6.gd: hold the fire, or break camp and ride). Today that stop is Act 3's rock shelter on the desert road, for both the desert and the coast return. Simon wants a new place.

What the stop has to do, whichever place it is:
- **One night, one fight:** 12 servants, a few at a time, from more than one side; the party holds round the fire; anyone downed must be helped up (act6.gd `_servants`).
- **Companions at their jobs** before the fight (CampJobs: the watch, the horses or the trucks, the cook, the scholar), fighting from their posts when it starts.
- **The rapport stop:** the fire where people open up a little more (Simon's rule: slowly, at travel stops).
- **Both routes:** the desert and the coast return both stop here, or each stops at its own version of it.
- **Quick to read at night:** where the dark is, where the cover is, where they come from.

Each option below has what the place is, why it fits, a sketch, its size, cover and approaches, where the jobs and the fire go, and the models it needs.

---

## Option A: The ruined monastery (Deir el-Rumi, a placeholder name)

**What it is.** An abandoned Coptic monastery in a shallow wadi, of the kind that stood on the old desert tracks between the Nile valley and the coast: a mud-brick enclosure wall, a squat keep (the *qasr*, the monks' refuge from raiders), a small domed church, rows of cells, a well with its wheel, and the monks' cemetery outside the wall. Both return routes pass it: the tracks to the desert crossing and to the coast part just beyond its gate.

**Why it fits.**
- **A refuge built for exactly this:** a walled place meant to be held through a night against what comes out of the desert. The keep's roof is the obvious last stand.
- **The night attack reads clearly:** the enclosure has one gate and two breaches where the wall has fallen, so the servants come from three known places; and from the cemetery outside the east wall, where the old graves give up their own dead to swell their ranks.
- **The rapport fire is intimate:** in the courtyard, under the church's dome, with the cells' doorways round it. Companions talk about faith, death and what they saw in the tomb.
- **Period-accurate:** many such monasteries stood empty or nearly so by the 1920s, their walls still standing.

**Sketch** (about 60 × 50 m inside the wall, in about 300 × 300 m of desert and wadi):

```
                    N
        . . . dunes . . . . . . . . . . . . .
     +--------------------======-----------+ +-------+
     | cells  cells  cells     breach (N)  | |       |
     |                                     | | ceme- |
     |   [church]        (fire)    [keep]  |=| tery  |  breach (E)
     |   (dome)          courtyard  roof   | |  + +  |  onto the graves
     |                    well(o)  ladder  | | +  +  |
     | cells  refectory   horses / trucks  | |       |
     +------------[ GATE ]-----------------+ +-------+
                     |
          the track: the coast road  /  the desert crossing
```

**Size, cover, approaches.**
- **Approaches:** the gate (S), the north breach and the east breach onto the cemetery. Servants come in by these three, a few at a time, and from the cemetery's graves once the fight is on.
- **Cover:** the well's wall, the church's porch columns, the cells' doorways and the stacked loads. The keep's roof (a ladder up) looks over all three ways in.
- **The dark:** outside the wall it is total. Inside, the fire and the lanterns in the cells light the courtyard, so the threat comes out of the breaches into the light.

**The jobs** (CampJobs spots):
- The watch on the keep's roof.
- The horses (desert) or the trucks (coast) inside the gate.
- The cook at the refectory hearth.
- The scholar in the church, copying the Coptic graffiti; a quiet rapport moment, and a line about the cemetery's odd graves.
- A second watch at the north breach.

**The fire** is in the middle of the courtyard, by the well.

**Models.**
- **New:** the enclosure wall in pieces, straight, corner and gate; a collapsed wall section; the keep; the domed church; a row of cells; the well with its wheel (*saqiya*); grave markers and low tomb-houses.
- **Count:** about 8–10 models (image-to-3d: about 2 GPU holds).
- **Blockout from existing kit:**
  - Walls, keep and cells as boxes.
  - The Cairo kit's door arches and mosque wall pieces for the church front.
  - Crates, bedrolls, tents, lanterns and the campfire as they are.
  - Palms (Palms) at the well.
  - The cemetery's graves as small boxes.
  - Playable in a day or two.

## Option B: The Bedouin well camp

**What it is.** A small spring and well in a fold of the desert, a few date palms, and the black goat-hair tents of a Bedouin family who water their camels there. They take you in for the night by the law of the desert, and they know the old stories of what walks after dark.

**Why it fits.**
- **Water for the animals:** the desert route needs the horses watered; it is a real reason to stop.
- **Hosts:** a host family gives the rapport fire a different colour. Companions behave in front of strangers; the old man's stories prompt their own.
- **The night attack comes at people who aren't yours:** the family's tents are on the edge of the dark. Do you hold the fire or go out to them? A choice in the fight. (Simon's settled choices allow forced outcomes like this.)

**Sketch** (about 80 × 60 m of palms and sand in a ring of low dunes):

```
              dunes (the dark: everywhere, but they come over the crests)
        ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
      ~    [tent]   [tent]          palms  *  *     ~
     ~        hosts' fire                 *          ~
     ~    camels (lines)      (o) well      *        ~
     ~                  (your fire)   saddles, loads ~
      ~   horses / trucks         [your tents]      ~
        ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ the track ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~
```

**Size, cover, approaches.**
- **Approaches:** open on every side, but the dunes' crests and the gaps between them shape where the servants come over: four or five lines in.
- **Cover:** palm trunks, the well's wall, the stacked saddles and loads, the camels kneeling in their lines. The tents are no cover at all.
- **The hard part:** the hosts' tents sit nearer the dark than your fire.

**The jobs.**
- The watch on the highest dune.
- The horses or trucks by the track.
- The cook sharing the hosts' fire, with the coffee ritual (a rapport moment).
- The scholar listening to the old man's stories.

**The fire** is yours, next to theirs.

**Models.**
- **New:** the goat-hair tent (2 sizes); the well; the hosts (2–3 people, image-to-3d); camel saddles; waterskins.
- **Count:** about 5–6 models.
- **Blockout from existing kit:** much of it exists already.
  - Palms.
  - Camels from the crowd's beasts.
  - DesertCamp's and BeachCamp's pieces.
  - Dunes from DesertRoute.
  - Playable in about a day.

**The catch.** The family is a new set of characters to write and to protect (or not), and that is story Simon may not want on a "race back".

## Option C: The necropolis edge and the old dig-house

**What it is.** The edge of a Late Period necropolis where the desert meets the escarpment: a field of robbed burial shafts, the stumps of mastabas, rock-cut tomb fronts in a low cliff. Above them is the mud-brick dig-house of an expedition that left in a hurry thirty years ago, with a walled yard and a roof terrace.

**Why it fits.**
- **The most on-theme:** the guardian's servants are the dead, and here the ground is full of them. In the fight they come out of the shafts as well as the dark: a threat from below, not only from outside the light.
- **The dig-house is a mirror of the party:** an earlier expedition, its notes left behind (a lore find, and a rapport prompt: "this could be us").
- **Clear defensive geometry:** the yard wall and the terrace.

**Sketch** (the dig-house yard about 25 × 20 m; the necropolis about 150 × 100 m below it):

```
          the escarpment: rock-cut tomb fronts  [] [] []  []
        ------------------------------------------------------
           o   o     o  shafts   o     o    o   (they climb out)
         [mastaba]     o     [mastaba]     o
              o    o       o         o
      +--------- yard wall ---------+
      |  dig-house   | (fire)  yard |   the track
      |  [terrace ^] |  loads       |  ======>
      +-----[ gate ]----------------+
```

**Size, cover, approaches.**
- **Approaches:** the shaft field (north, wide open, servants rising from the shafts nearest the yard), the track (east) and the escarpment's tombs. Spawns are at holes you can see, so you can watch them.
- **Cover:** the yard wall (chest-high), the dig-house doorway, the terrace parapet, the mastaba stumps out in the field.
- **The hard part:** a shaft can open inside the yard.

**The jobs.**
- The watch on the terrace.
- The horses or trucks in the yard.
- The cook in the dig-house kitchen.
- The scholar reading the old expedition's notebooks (a find).

**The fire** is in the yard.

**Models.**
- **New:** the dig-house (a single building, Cairo-ish mud brick, with a terrace); shaft openings; mastaba stumps; rock-cut tomb fronts.
- **Count:** about 5–7 models.
- **Blockout from existing kit:**
  - The dungeon kit's glyph walls and pillars make the tomb fronts.
  - Boxes for the mastabas.
  - The Cairo kit for the dig-house front.
  - The shafts are holes with a lip.
  - Playable in about a day.
- **Code:** shaft spawns are a small addition to act6.gd's `_servant`: a spawn at a hole, a climb-out animation, or a fade.

**The catch.** It's another set of tombs and the dead, after Acts 4 and 5 were full of them. It's less of a change of scene on the way home.

## Recommendation: A, the monastery, with C's best idea

The monastery gives the night fight the clearest shape:
- **Three known ways in** and a refuge built for exactly this. The keep's roof is a last stand you can see from the moment you arrive.
- **A real change of scene:** stone, faith and a Christian cemetery, after a whole act of pharaonic tombs.
- **Fits both returns:** it stands where the two routes part.

Take C's best idea into it: the monks' cemetery outside the east wall gives up its dead in the fight. That gives:
- a threat that's visibly on its way, and close;
- an eerie note: even the Christian dead answer the guardian;
- a scholar's line in the church about the graves.

**Cost.** The blockout reuses what exists, and the models are about 8–10 pieces, two GPU holds. The fight code stays act6.gd's own, with spawn points instead of a ring: the two breaches, the gate and the graves.

**If Simon prefers people to places,** B is the warmest: the hosts make the rapport fire different. It brings new characters, though.

**Open questions for Simon:**
1. **One place or two:** one place both routes share, or the coast its own version (a ruined lighthouse fort on the coast, the same idea)?
2. **Breaking camp to ride on:** should the option stay? At the monastery, riding out through the gate under attack could be its own scene.
